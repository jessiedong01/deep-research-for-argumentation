\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Two evaluations compare \sys{} with OpenAI Deep Research \citep{openai2025deepresearch} and STORM \citep{shao2024storm}. The first is interactive and consists of automated debates between \sys{} and Deep Research, scored by a language model. The second is a blind human assessment of full reports written by all three systems. Both evaluations are small, and their size is stated here and discussed further in \Cref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{Automated debates.}
Three motions were chosen to require different kinds of reasoning. The first is moral and states that moral systems rooted in theism are preferable to non-theistic ones. The second is economic and technical and states that U.S.\ semiconductor manufacturers will outperform their global peers over the next decade because of the CHIPS and Science Act. The third is conceptual and states that AI systems can be creative in the way humans are. Each motion was debated twice, with \sys{} and Deep Research exchanging the Pro and Con sides, which yields six transcripts and controls for any advantage of one side over the other. Each system researched the motion in its own way. \sys{} decomposed the motion into a DAG and built its case from the answered nodes, while Deep Research ran its default retrieval and synthesis process. The exchange itself was generated by GPT-5-mini, which wrote alternating turns of 200 to 300 words for each side from that side's research and the opponent's previous turn.

GPT-4o-mini then scored each transcript on stance consistency, defined as the fraction of turns that hold the assigned side, and on logical coherence, evidence integration, rebuttal specificity, persuasiveness, and fluency, each on a scale from 1 to 10. The scoring followed the framework of DeepResearch Bench, an unpublished course project by Abhinav Chinta and Charles Ding.

\paragraph{Human assessment of reports.}
Ten motions were selected so that each requires evidence synthesis and a position rather than factual recall, and together they span ethics, economics, policy, and technology. \Cref{tab:reports} lists them. Each system wrote one full report per motion. One author scored the thirty reports with the identity of the system hidden. The rubric measured the supported-claims rate, defined as the fraction of claims that carry a citation, the evidence utilization, defined as the fraction of cited sources that the argument actually uses, the quality of logical connections on a scale from 1 to 10, and whether the report holds a firm position throughout. The rubric also included evidence relevance and originality, but the recorded scores for these two measures were identical across the three systems and could not be reconstructed, so they are omitted. Because there is a single rater, no agreement statistic can be computed, and the values reported below are means over ten reports without confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Retained materials.}
The \sys{} and STORM reports for the ten motions are available in the repository, together with the prompts, the pipeline, and one complete logged run. The Deep Research reports and the debate transcripts were not retained. \Cref{sec:analysis,sec:citations} therefore analyze the twenty retained reports and the logged run.
