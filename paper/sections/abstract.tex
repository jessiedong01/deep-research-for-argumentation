Deep research systems answer a question by searching the web over many steps and writing a report with citations. Leading systems such as OpenAI Deep Research and STORM are built to summarize the evidence on every side, yet many tasks call for an argument that commits to a position. \sys{} is a deep research pipeline for argument generation. It decomposes a question into a graph of sub-questions, answers the narrowest of them with web search, and composes the answers upward into a report that argues for a single position. In a blind evaluation on ten contested questions, \sys{} took a firm position in \humStanceDC{} of 10 reports, compared with \humStanceOAI{} for OpenAI Deep Research and \humStanceStorm{} for STORM. It also attached citations to a larger share of its claims (\humSupportedDC{} against \humSupportedOAI{} and \humSupportedStorm{}) and received the highest score for logical coherence (\humLogicDC{} out of 10 against \humLogicOAI{} and \humLogicStorm{}). Compared with STORM, its reports use less than half as many hedging words (\txHedgeDc{} against \txHedgeSt{} per 1{,}000 words). An automatic citation check, which identifies the correct source for \atValBestCitedPct\% of claims when citations are known to be right, finds that the cited source is the best match for only \atEvalBestCitedPct\% of claims in the evaluated reports, close to the \atEvalBestChancePct\% expected by chance.
