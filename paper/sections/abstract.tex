Searching the web in an iterative process to answer a question and produce a cited report, leading systems such as OpenAI Deep Research and STORM summarize the evidence for each side but do not take a position. We present \sys{}, a deep research system designed to generate arguments, which decomposes a question into a graph of sub-questions, answering each narrow query on the web, and combining the answers for each sub-question into an argumentative report that takes a position. In blind evaluation on 10 contentious questions, \sys{} takes a firm position in \humStanceDC{} reports, compared to \humStanceOAI{} for OpenAI Deep Research and \humStanceStorm{} for STORM. In six automated debates against OpenAI Deep Research, it holds its assigned side in \autoStanceDC\% of turns (vs.\ \autoStanceOAI\%).

\sys{} cites a higher fraction of claims (\humSupportedDC, vs.\ \humSupportedOAI{} and \humSupportedStorm), uses a larger share of its cited sources (\humUtilDC, vs.\ \humUtilOAI{} and \humUtilStorm), achieves the highest logical coherence score (\humLogicDC{} out of 10, vs.\ \humLogicOAI{} and \humLogicStorm) and uses fewer hedging words than STORM (\txHedgeDc{} per 1k words vs.\ \txHedgeSt) and more commitment words (\txCommitDc{} vs.\ \txCommitSt). Its reports are shorter than STORM's (median \txWordsDc{} words vs.\ \txWordsSt) and cite more densely (\txCiteDensityDc{} vs.\ \txCiteDensitySt{} citations per 100 words), but draw on fewer distinct sources (\txSourcesDc{} vs.\ \txSourcesSt). An automatic check of the fidelity of citations (which picks out the correct source for \atValBestCitedPct\% of known-correct claims) finds that in our 10 evaluated reports, cited sources are the best match for only \atEvalBestCitedPct\%, near the \atEvalBestChancePct\% expected by chance. We trace the likely cause to how citation numbers are handled when answers are combined, and giving each source a single global number restores the best match to \atFixBestCitedPct\% in a replay of a logged run.
